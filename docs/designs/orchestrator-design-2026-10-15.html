Design document · 2026-10-15 · Draft v4.4 for review

An orchestrator profile for IDE research on native Hermes

One orchestrator and N researchers, coordinated through native Hermes Kanban, delivered from the Metta-AI/ide-distributions repository.

Pinned sources: Hermes v0.21.6 (commit 818c13be, the version on the Softmax hosting image), metta main at 639fb19614 (pulled 2026-10-15 13:32 PDT), and ide-mvp main at 15babd49. Aaron's softmax-ide pins Hermes v0.21.7 (71d34bad). This design is not made against that version.

Executive Summary

Today the autoresearch profile does the research. It talks to the IDE through a file inbox and outbox, and the bridge relays each message word for word. This document replaces that transport, and most of the harness around it, with native Hermes features. A new orchestrator profile owns the IDE conversation and each IDE-facing record. N profiles named researcher-<i> do the work. All of them install from one researcher distribution, the renamed autoresearch. They coordinate through Kanban cards on one board. Each researcher has its own clone of one bare canonical repository per game, and it works each card in its own git worktree. The board, the canonical repository, and one small table of handled events in plugin storage hold all state that must survive. Thus a gateway restart, a sandbox restart or a lost session loses nothing. Hermes wakes the orchestrator when a card ends. A one-minute supervisor cron also wakes it, but that cron costs a model call only while an event is still unacknowledged.

The simplicity decision of James (J-H) gave the final version its shape: no gates, permissions, infrastructure or defensive code that adds no functionality. The harness shrinks to deterministic tools plus a two-rule guard plugin of about 60 lines. That plugin replaces a 1,641-line policy file. The action ledger, the integration receipts, the signals framework and deferred work are all cut. All spend management is removed, which is an accepted risk (K3). Hosting needs six items from Rohit (R-1 to R-6), and two of them are verifications. This is v4.4. Codex attacked v4 in round 6 ("breaks as specified"), and each claim was verified again in source. Round 7 returned PARTIAL and gave v4.2. Then v4.3 was a simplicity pass, and Codex round 8 cut more. One recurrence that the cuts reopen is recorded (K11). The new orchestrator code is not written yet, thus the line counts here are not a measured final count. Two items stay unverified until a hosted smoke test: whether lines from the orchestrator show in the IDE coach chat like the lines of the bridge, and whether the card subscription row carries the destination of the bridge session. Four decisions wait on James (section 20). The largest is approval of the migration and of the private repository Metta-AI/ide-distributions.

1 · Problem

  • The IDE user talks to one agent and expects research to occur behind it: experiments on the shared game, a champion that improves, progress in plain language, and a way to steer.
  • Today the research profile talks through a file inbox and outbox. The bridge relays each message word for word, thus the user sees raw agent text.
  • The bridge carries protocol that it must not know.
  • About 60% of the runtime directory, all of the comms and queue code, and most of the plugin are harness for cross-agent steps.
  • No agent owns the IDE-facing picture, answers the questions of researchers, or decides what to research next from the point of view of the IDE.

The user-visible goal is simple. A person in the IDE talks to one agent. Behind that agent, research operates on a shared game, and a champion policy improves over time. The person wants progress in plain language and a way to steer.

The current implementation reaches that goal through plumbing that the user must never see. The autoresearch profile writes outbound messages to files. The bridge relays each one of them to the chat word for word, as a title, a blank line and a body. The bridge also forwards IDE coach tasks into the inbox files of the profile, and it closes IDE tasks when a note answers them.1 Each cross-agent step inside the profile (admission of work, routing, acknowledgement, publication) is harness code that the profile carries. The inventory puts that at about 60% of the runtime directory, all of the comms and queue code, and most of the plugin.2

As a result, nobody is responsible for the whole picture. The research profile is busy with the harness. The bridge relays text that it did not write and does not understand. No agent speaks to the user in its own words, answers the questions of researchers, or decides what to research next with the needs of the IDE in view. Section 4 describes the shape that repairs this.

2.1 What v4.3 and v4.4 removed for simplicity

Each cut below comes with the one-line reason why it is safe to drop. The second group came from Codex round 8.

  • The action ledger with reservation states, owner liveness, frozen texts and lock order. Each orchestrator action is already idempotent by a key (the card key, a say key derived from the event, promotion stages on canonical). Thus two sessions that handle one event make one card, one message and one submit without a ledger.
  • Acknowledgements keyed by board generation. On a board rebuild, the handled table is cleared. Nothing else needs it.
  • Create unassigned, subscribe, then assign. The wake is a latency optimization, and the supervisor is the guarantee. Thus a card that ends before its subscription lands is picked up a minute later.
  • Guard rules for completion-with-receipt, write confinement, sleep loops, worker-entry receipt checks, integrations/ reservation and receipt lineage validation. The orchestrator already sees a completed card with no merge, and it integrates that card. Sleep loops are spend (J-5). A retried card that was already integrated does duplicate work, and integrate turns that work into a no-op.
  • Admission checks beyond the two data prerequisites (a noise record for an experiment, and no second open card with the same entity). The rest is doctrine.
  • Repair-card admission exceptions, session liveness timeouts and lock order go with the items above.
  • (Codex round 8) The integration receipt file. Git already answers "is this card merged". The existing merge code skips a result that canonical history contains.3 Thus "integrated" means "the commit of the card branch is an ancestor of main".
  • (Codex round 8) The signals framework. status, the records and the champion check cover what the orchestrator asks. No incident shows that the thrash and plateau thresholds corrected anything.
  • (Codex round 8) Deferred work (control/deferred.json, due-time release). No settled requirement asks for scheduled future work. Pending cards and pause remain.
  • (Codex round 8) The noise-record check in card_create. The retained stats design tool already refuses an absent or wrong-version noise record.4
  • (Codex round 8) card_request_changes, per-kind runtime tiers, the CI equality check on generated copies, the launcher's second parser of the gate output, the --rebuild command, a duplicate deny list on the orchestrator, and the separate X-Cogent-Agent identity story.

3 · Goals and Non-Goals

  • Seven goals (G1 to G7) for this version: one orchestrator per sandbox, N researchers, Kanban-only communication, durability, researcher-to-orchestrator wakes, a harness reduced to tools plus one guard, and promotion owned by the orchestrator.
  • Six explicit non-goals (N1 to N6). They include spend management, new IDE panels, cross-sandbox research and any change to Hermes itself.
  • Exactly-once wake delivery is a non-goal. Hermes does not give it, thus the design acknowledges events durably and reconciles instead.

4 · The Shape

  • One sandbox, one Hermes root: the default gateway profile of hosting, one orchestrator, N researcher-<i>, and one bare repository per game.
  • The orchestrator makes cards through a checked card_create: one named researcher per card, a worktree on the clone of that researcher, per-card skills, an idempotency key and a retry limit.
  • Researchers end each card with the deterministic workspace integrate tool, and then they complete or block the card. "Integrated" is a git fact (the card branch is an ancestor of main).
  • Terminal card events wake the session that made the card. A one-minute cron covers comments and all other events that are still unacknowledged.
  • The orchestrator speaks to the user only through ide_say (background turns) or its normal reply (bridge turns). Researchers have no IDE tools.

The sandbox holds one Hermes root. Inside it are the default gateway profile of hosting, one orchestrator profile, N researcher-<i> profiles installed from the same researcher distribution, and one bare git repository per game at <root>/canonical-<game>.git. Each researcher clones that repository. The IDE bridge posts chat lines to the API-server session of the orchestrator. The orchestrator plans the research and makes Kanban cards on the autoresearch board through its own checked card_create tool. Each card names one researcher. It carries a worktree workspace on the clone of that researcher (fast-forwarded to canonical main first), per-card skills, an idempotency key and a retry limit. The dispatcher claims the card and operates it as hermes -p researcher-<i>.

The researcher works in its worktree, and it operates the deterministic workspace integrate tool as its last step. That tool pushes the card branch, validates, merges under the shared lock and pushes main. The card then ends with kanban_complete (handoff summary plus metadata) or kanban_block (a question, or an integrate failure). Terminal card events wake the orchestrator session that made the card. In the middle of a card, researchers write comments.

A one-minute cron on the orchestrator profile operates a gate script. The script wakes a supervisor turn while a board event or comment is still unacknowledged, or while XP, credits, permits or the champion changed. Each orchestrator turn starts with board_reconcile, which lists unacknowledged events. An event is acknowledged only after the action that handles it succeeds. The orchestrator talks to the user through ide_say (background turns) or its normal reply (bridge turns). It updates coach tasks through ide_task_update. Researchers have no IDE tools.

5 · Repo Layout, Install and Update

  • One repository, Metta-AI/ide-distributions, holds both distributions plus an ontology/ folder, a shared/ package, and a tools/sync_shared.py script. The script copies the shared files into each distribution (the copies are generated).
  • Hermes can install only a whole local folder. It cannot select a ref or a subfolder from a URL. Thus each distribution must install and test alone.
  • Install from a clean checkout where nothing has run. The installer rejects symlinks and copies directories whole, thus a stray .venv either breaks the install or ships.
  • Hosting keeps its mechanism (a local absolute path) and its tree-checked git archive. Thus no credential enters the image.

Why the layout looks like this

hermes profile install accepts a git URL. It makes a shallow clone of the default branch, and it requires distribution.yaml at the clone root. It has no syntax for a ref, a branch or a subfolder.67 A local directory is used in place and copied. That works for any path, and a subfolder of a clone is one such path. The installed manifest records the source, the name, the version and the install time, but no commit.8 hermes profile update stages the recorded source again. Thus for a local path, an update is a git pull of the clone and then the Hermes update.9

That leaves one shape that works: both distributions live in one repository as sibling folders, and each one installs from a local path. The installer copies one folder and nothing else. Thus each shared item must be inside each folder at install time. This is why the layout has a sync script and a vendored copy of the shared package (section 7.6), not a path or git dependency.

ide-distributions/
  README.md                      lists distributions, install and update
  .github/workflows/ci.yml       matrix: one job per distribution
  .gitignore, LICENSE
  ontology/ONTOLOGY.md           the single shared ontology (copied into each distribution)
  shared/ide_research_core/      shared Python package (copied into each distribution)
  tools/sync_shared.py           performs the copies (run before a commit)
  orchestrator/                  distribution.yaml, pyproject.toml, uv.lock, tests/, README.md, docs/
  researcher/                    the renamed autoresearch; same file set
  docs/designs/                  design documents, including this one

Per-folder lint configuration stays in the pyproject.toml of each distribution, because the autoresearch folder already pins its own. The shared pieces at repository level are the CI workflow, the root README, and the ontology and shared package that the sync script distributes.

Install and update on a manual host

Install from a clean checkout in which nothing has run uv sync or tests. The installer stages the live directory, rejects each symlink in it, and copies selected directories whole. Thus a .venv or __pycache__ in the source either fails the install or ships.10 Keep a separate development clone.

git clone https://github.com/Metta-AI/ide-distributions.git ~/ide-distributions
hermes profile install ~/ide-distributions/researcher --name researcher-1 --yes
hermes profile install ~/ide-distributions/researcher --name researcher-2 --yes
hermes profile install ~/ide-distributions/orchestrator --name orchestrator --yes

# update
git -C ~/ide-distributions pull --ff-only
hermes profile update researcher-1 && hermes profile update researcher-2 && hermes profile update orchestrator

An update keeps config.yaml. --force-config resets it.

Hosting

The hosting mechanism does not change: a local absolute path. The source constant in the image-build module becomes Metta-AI/ide-distributions. The lock file pins a commit of the new repository. The --ide-mvp option of the build script becomes --repo. The archive carries both trees (orchestrator/ and researcher/) with one commit marker each. The repository is private for now (the comment of James on 2026-10-14). Thus the tree-checked git archive step stays exactly as it is, and no credential goes into the image.1112 Section 16 records the alternatives that lost.

The researcher-guard plugin

The guard replaces the 1,641-line policy plugin with about 60 lines: a pre-tool hook with two rules. Each rule is tied to a failure that can corrupt shared state. Codex round 6 (finding E) showed why the deny list cannot do this job. Dispatcher-owned, non-delegated workers automatically get the native kanban toolset, which includes kanban_create, kanban_link and kanban_schedule.20 And the YAML deny patterns match shell commands only, not Python tools.21

All other parts of the old policy go: write confinement, completion gating and loop detection. 7 · The Orchestrator Distribution

  • The orchestrator is the research lead for one player on one game. It decides what to research, writes cards, answers researchers, judges promotions, and is the only voice to the user.
  • It has one plugin toolset and nothing else: no terminal, file, code, web or native kanban toolset. Thus each board action goes through the checked tools.
  • Durable decisions live on the canonical repository. Plugin storage holds one table of handled events and nothing else.
  • From the IDE, it accepts exactly what the bridge sends today. Each other action arrives as a pre-formatted chat line.
  • The ontology and the shared code reach it through a sync script, because the installer copies one folder.

7.1 Identity and knowledge

The SOUL.md describes the research lead for one player on one game. It talks to the user in plain words (the communication doctrine), decides what to research next, writes cards, and answers the questions of researchers with its own authority (J-E). It judges promote decisions. It never relays researcher text to the user unless asked (J-F). It reports from the board and the canonical repository, never from memory of a previous turn.

The SOUL also carries an output-ownership rule from Codex round 6 (finding C) and round 7 (N6).2526 The rule is cooperative, not enforced. It keys on how the turn entered, not on the chat role, because a native wake also arrives as a user message.27 A turn that started with a coach chat line from the bridge replies normally and does not call ide_say. The bridge publishes the final reply itself, thus a call to ide_say as well says it twice.28 A turn that started with a Kanban notification or the supervisor prompt has no reader. Hermes stores the reply of a wake in session history and discards it.29 Thus in such a turn, all text for the user goes through ide_say. Test T6 covers a bridge reply and then a wake in one session.

Beside the SOUL are the shared ontology/ONTOLOGY.md (section 7.5) and doctrine/orchestrator.md, about 40 rules. These are the rules that the old admission module enforced in code. Now they are doctrine that the orchestrator obeys. card_create checks only one of them: no second open card with the same entity key (section 7.2). The rest:

  • No experiment card before a noise record exists for the game version (C10).
  • At most one planning card open (C11).
  • Each ref in a card body must resolve.
  • No second open card on the same hypothesis or experiment.
  • A tool-fault: block is never retried without a repair (D-26).
  • A card whose branch is already merged is never rerun.
  • A repair card outranks new experiments on the same entity.

The game knowledge is a short games/<game>/GAME.md summary (bindings, outcome metrics, league id, minimum episodes).

7.2 Tools

The orchestrator enables one toolset, orchestrator (a plugin), on each platform it operates on: platform_toolsets.api_server: [orchestrator] and platform_toolsets.cron: [orchestrator]. Cron agent jobs operate on platform cron, not cli.3031 It has no terminal, file, code, web or native kanban toolset.

There are three reasons to leave out the native kanban toolset. It shows a raw kanban_create beside the checked wrapper, thus an ordinary tool choice bypasses admission.32 The native model tool drops branch_name and max_retries.33 And kanban_unblock carries no answer.34 The board tools of the plugin call the Kanban database API in process, the same API that the native tools use, and they add the checks.

The plugin is a stdlib shim inside the Hermes process, the same pattern as the plugin of today. It operates each tool as a subprocess of the Python in the virtualenv of the profile, with JSON in and out. Thus the dependencies of the package live in the venv that the launcher syncs.15 The exception is a board tool that must share the Kanban database connection of Hermes. Those tools operate in process through the importable Hermes Kanban module.

8 · Communication and Durability

  • All traffic between the orchestrator and the researchers is Kanban: cards, comments, block and unblock, and completion handoffs.
  • Terminal card events wake the session that made the card. Comments never wake anyone natively, thus a one-minute supervisor with a no-cost quiet path covers them.
  • Delivery is checkpointed, not exactly-once. An event is acknowledged only after the action that handles it succeeds, and each turn starts with a list of unacknowledged events.
  • Many sessions can act at the same time. Each action is idempotent by a key, thus two sessions that handle one event make one card, one message and one submit.
  • The restart and loss matrix at the end of this section lists what survives each failure.

Figure 2 follows one card through its life. It includes a mid-card comment that the supervisor finds and a steer sent back to the worker. The subsections explain each mechanism and where Hermes helps or falls short.

8.3 Who wakes whom, natively

  • Card terminal events wake the session that made the card, if that session has an api_server destination. Thus cards made in a bridge turn or a wake turn wake that session.686925
  • Cards made in a supervisor (cron) turn have no wake destination. Cron clears the routing context, and the auto-subscribe of Hermes returns false when there is no persistent channel.70 Their events reach the orchestrator through the next supervisor tick. Comments, status edits, archive and unblock never wake anyone.
  • Delivery is checkpointed, not exactly-once.71 Thus the unacknowledged-event model recovers a lost wake within a tick.
  • A session that no longer exists cannot resume, and its subscriptions do not move to a new session.72 Sessions live in state.db, thus this is rare, and the supervisor covers it.
  • Hermes stores the reply text of a wake turn in session history. That text never reaches the bridge, which polls only the run it submitted. That is why background turns use ide_say (section 7.1).

8.4 The supervisor

The cron agent job supervisor operates every minute with script: supervisor_gate.py. The scheduler operates the script before the model step. Only a final JSON line with "wakeAgent": false stops the turn. Hermes wakes the agent on each other output, malformed output included.7374 The script operates from the scripts/ directory of the profile with the scoped HERMES_HOME and the passthrough secrets.75 It opens the board autoresearch by name under the shared root and the plugin database, and it checks:

  1. Any task_events or task_comments row that is not in handled.
  2. XP requests that the orchestrator waits on and that reached a terminal status (a platform read).
  3. Credits available again for experiments parked on awaiting.credits.
  4. Permit asks that were answered (awaiting.permit).
  5. Every 10th tick, the champion versus the league (leaderboard).

When nothing is pending, the script prints {"wakeAgent": false}, and the tick costs no model call. The 446 empty wakes of PlayerADE are the failure this prevents (section 15). Otherwise it prints {"wakeAgent": true, "context": {...}}, and Hermes puts the context before the prompt as text.76 The agent turn (platform cron, toolset orchestrator) starts with board_reconcile, acts, acknowledges, and ends.

Hermes skips a tick while the same job still operates.77 That prevents two supervisors at the same time only. It does not prevent overlap with bridge or wake turns (section 8.5). One minute is the polling interval, not a recovery bound. A skipped tick or a failed turn delays the action to the next tick that still finds the event unacknowledged.

8.5 Many sessions, one state

There are four kinds of orchestrator session: the fixed IDE session of the bridge, new IDE sessions (J-5), supervisor cron sessions, and sessions that a wake resumed. Two sessions can handle the same event (a wake and a supervisor tick). Each action is idempotent by a key, thus that is harmless:

  • A card, by its application key (plus the short create lock in card_create).
  • A say, by a key derived from the event id (the server accepts one text per key and rejects a second, which is a no-op for the user).
  • A submit, by the stages of the promotion record on canonical (section 11).
  • An acknowledgement, by the handled table.

The rule is: act, then acknowledge. No lock is held across a model turn, and nothing else is necessary.

9 · Workspace Model and the Repair Path

  • One bare canonical repository per game, one clone per researcher, and one worktree per card on a card/<id> branch.
  • Integrate is the deterministic last step of each card. It pushes the branch first, then it validates, merges under a lock and pushes main.
  • "Integrated" is a git fact: the commit of the card branch is an ancestor of main. A crash between push and completion leaves a merged branch. The orchestrator completes that card and does not rerun it.
  • When integrate fails, the researcher blocks the card. The orchestrator then makes a repair card cut from the pushed commit. This is the form of delegation that the orchestrator uses (J-6).

9.1 Layout

  • One bare canonical repository per game: <root>/canonical-<game>.git. Hosting makes it in prepare, seeded from games/<game>/seed. Its main holds the knowledge base, plan.md, records, strategy/STRATEGY.md, control/pause.json and promotion records.
  • The $PROFILE/workspace of each researcher is a clone with main checked out and never edited there. Cards use worktrees on card/<id> branches under <clone>/.worktrees/<id>. card_create fast-forwards the main of the clone before the card exists (section 7.2). Thus the base of the worktree is canonical main at creation time, recorded as base_sha.
  • The orchestrator has its own clone, used for reads, plan.md, control/ and promotion records. A shared checkout with a researcher brings back the shared-file collisions seen in the transcripts.41

9.2 Integrate: the deterministic last step of every card

workspace integrate combines what today are the card-finish, merge and provenance modules. It does these steps in order:

  1. Commit the worktree.
  2. Push card/<id> to the canonical first, thus recovery is branch-based whatever occurs next.
  3. Validate every changed record at the result commit against the mutability rules.
  4. Run the overlap check on the same entity against main since base_sha (C4).
  5. Take canonical.lock in the bare repository (one host, shared by all researchers and the orchestrator).
  6. Merge.
  7. Regenerate kb/index.md and the other generated views.
  8. Push main.
  9. Release the lock, and print {card_sha, main_sha}.

"Integrated" is a git fact: the commit of the card branch is an ancestor of canonical main. The existing merge code already checks this to skip a result that history contains.3 A crash between the push and kanban_complete leaves a merged branch that board_reconcile sees. Thus the orchestrator completes the card (card_close(done)). Hermes can retry such a card first, because its retry knows only failure counts.45 Then the first step of the card skill sees the merged branch and completes at once. A retry that misses that step does duplicate work, and integrate skips that work. A researcher card cannot change strategy/STRATEGY.md, control/ or promotion records (D-25). Integrate refuses them.

9.3 The failure path (J-6)

Integrate can fail at validation (step 3), the overlap check (4), the merge (6) or the push of main (9). Then the tool prints the reason, and card/<id> is already pushed. The sequence is then:

  1. The researcher calls kanban_block("integrate: <reason>").
  2. The blocked event wakes the orchestrator, or the supervisor finds the event.
  3. The orchestrator makes a repair card with card_create(kind="repair", ...). There is no parent link. A link to the original, which is still blocked, gates the repair forever.6643 The body carries the key of the failed card, the reason, the pushed branch and card_sha. The branch of the repair is repair/<id>. card_create makes it from card_sha (a git branch in the clone of the assignee), thus the worktree step of the dispatcher finds the existing branch.81 The assignee is a free researcher, or the researcher of the original once its worker is gone. The entity key of the repair is its own, thus no admission exception is necessary. Its priority is above new experiments on that entity.
  4. The repair worker rebases or edits, operates integrate, and completes.
  5. The original stays blocked until the repair branch is merged. Then the orchestrator does card_close(original, archived) and records the lineage in the handoff of the repair.

Why a card and not delegate_task: the orchestrator has no terminal. A delegate_task child inherits the toolset of the parent, and it is not durable across a restart.82

A safety net backs this up. board_reconcile flags a completed card whose branch is not merged. The orchestrator then does workspace_integrate(card) from the pushed branch. If that fails, it takes the repair path.

9.4 Shared game, N researchers

card_create refuses a second open card on the same hypothesis or experiment. The planning card (one at a time, C11) splits topics across researchers. A researcher sees the integrated claims of others at its next card, because its clone is fast-forwarded at card creation.

11 · Promotion as an Orchestrator Task

  • The card of a researcher ends with a promote decision and a candidate version. The orchestrator decides, submits and tells the user.
  • Submission obeys a write-ahead order, thus an interrupted submit is never replayed blindly.
  • A champion conflict pauses research through control_write(pause) and tells the user.

Promotion is a special task that the orchestrator starts (J-4). The flow is:

  1. The card of a researcher ends with experiment: {id, status: reviewed, decision: promote} and the candidate version id. Its branch is merged into main.
  2. The orchestrator makes sure that the baseline equals the current champion (leaderboard) and that no promotion is in flight (promotions/ on main).
  3. promotion_submit(experiment) operates in write-ahead order:8384
    1. Commit and push the intent promotion record under the lock.
    2. Do the guarded softmax submit (refusals are champion_conflict, champion_unknown and baseline_not_champion, and nothing is made on a refusal).
    3. Commit submitted with the submission id.
    4. When the league shows it, record recorded and copy STRATEGY.md.
    5. Record published.
    An interrupted submit is never blindly replayed. The next reconcile reads the intent record plus the leaderboard and submission status, and continues from the stage the platform shows.
  4. ide_say in plain words: what was promoted and why.
  5. On a champion conflict (check (e) of the gate): control_write(pause), and tell the user. The lines resume research or bootstrap now clear it.

Bootstrap: on a fresh install with no champion, bootstrap_champion() (the starter policy) operates before an experiment card.
